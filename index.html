Financial Literacy for Teens04
04 • Smart spending

Before You Buy: Pause. Compare. Decide.

The $200 Shoes

You have $250 in your account. Your favorite shoes are on sale for $200 — today only.

Would you buy them?

Use the 24-hour rule

  • Wait before non-essential purchases.
  • Compare prices and alternatives.
  • Ask what you give up by spending the money.
  • Do not confuse a discount with a saving.

Opportunity cost = what you give up when you choose one option over another.

Credit Is Borrowed Money — Not Free Money

You purchase it with a credit card but cannot pay the full balance when the bill arrives.

→
📈 Interest

Credit score

APR / interest

Financial Literacy for Teens08
08 • Saving vs. investing

Let Time Work for You

Saving

Generally focuses on keeping money available for near-term needs and goals, with relatively low risk.

AccessStabilityShort term

Investing

Uses assets that can grow or lose value over time. Higher potential return usually comes with higher risk.

GrowthRiskLong term

Compound growth

Money can potentially earn returns, and those returns can themselves contribute to future growth. Starting earlier can provide more time for compounding to work.

Key concepts to learn: diversification, risk, fees, time horizon, and long-term investing. Avoid treating investing as guaranteed profit.

−
$12

=
$13

*Before other business expenses such as marketing, shipping, payment processing, taxes, or software.

Revenue

Money received from sales.

Costs

Money required to create and deliver the product or service.

Profit

What remains after relevant expenses are accounted for.

Business question: “If I sell 100 shirts, how much do I actually keep?”

Spot the Scam Before It Costs You

🚨 “You won!”

“Send $100 first to claim your $5,000 prize.”

🚨 “Easy money!”

“Work from home and earn $5,000 per week. No experience required.”

🚨 “Act now!”

“Your bank account is compromised. Click this link immediately.”

🚨 “Secret investment!”

“Guaranteed high returns with zero risk.”

🔍 Verify independently
⏸️ Slow down under pressure
🔐 Protect passwords & codes
💳 Never send money to claim money

12 • Case study

Meet Jordan: Put the Lessons Together

Jordan is 18

Income: $2,000/month • Spending: $1,800 • Savings: $50 • Credit-card debt: $3,000 • Emergency savings: $0 • Goal: buy a car and attend college

What should Jordan think about first?

1. Cash flow

Where can spending be adjusted without ignoring essential needs?

2. Debt

Understand interest, fees, and a realistic repayment plan.

3. Goals

Prioritize emergency savings and major goals based on timing and necessity.

Discussion point: Different people can make different reasonable choices. The important skill is evaluating consequences and priorities.

Financial Literacy for Teens13
13 • Decision framework

Use the S.T.O.P. Test

S
Do I really need it?Separate needs, wants, and short-term emotions.
T
What will it truly cost me?Consider price, interest, fees, maintenance, and opportunity cost.
O
What are my other options?Compare prices, alternatives, timing, and whether you can wait.
P
What happens if things go wrong?Think about emergencies, lost income, debt, or other downside risks.

Good financial decisions are rarely about “yes” or “no.” They are about understanding the consequences.
